Assembly and wiring guide

Slim USB controller with Raspberry Pico

A KY-023 joystick on the left, four 12×12 buttons on the right and a Raspberry Pi Pico inside a slim 23 mm shell, with the long grips of 90s gamepads. It connects with a USB cable and is recognised as a standard gamepad by PCs, Recalbox, RetroPie, Batocera and Android, without drivers.

PLA or PETG, 0.2 mm layers, 3 perimeters, 20% infill. Footprint 134 × 111 mm, 23 mm thick in the middle and 33 mm at the grip tips: it fits all Kobra printers and the i3 Mega S. Before printing, measure the hole spacing of your KY-023 (joy_fori, 20.5 × 26.5), the height of the metal bracket (joy_alt, 11.5), the thickness of the Pico board (pm_sp, 1.0) and the height of the USB-C socket (usb_h, 3.2), and correct the parameters in controller_pico.scad if needed.

The joystick pin marked +5V must go to the Pico's 3V3(OUT), not to VBUS or VSYS: with 5 V the VRx/VRy outputs exceed the 3.3 V the inputs can handle.

Pin numbers count from 1 (top left, next to the USB socket) going down the left side to 20, then back up the right side from 21 to 40. The joystick goes to the analogue ground AGND (pin 33) for less noise on the axes; the buttons to any GND (pins 3, 8, 13, 18, 23, 28, 38).

On 12×12 buttons the pins on the same side are already connected together: if a button always reads as pressed, you used two pins on the same side. Always take two diagonally opposite pins (1 and 4).

Steps

STEP 1

Prepare the parts

cutter2.5 mm drill bit
  1. Remove the supports from the grips and clean the centring lip of the bottom shell.
  2. Clean up the post pilot holes with the 2.5 mm bit (joystick, plate and the 6 closing posts).
  3. Dry fit: the button caps must slide in the square holes without forcing, and the two shells must close without steps.
STEP 2

Solder the wires and mount the joystick

KY-0234 × M3×65 wires, 15 cm
  1. Solder 5 wires, 15 cm long, to the joystick, directly onto the 90° pins (shorten them to 3–4 mm): Dupont connectors do not fit. Slide heat-shrink over each joint.
  2. Place the top shell upside down on the bench. Push the stick through the 26 mm hole on the left and rest the board on the 4 posts, with the connector towards the back.
  3. Drive in the M3×6 screws without overtightening: the plastic taps itself.
  4. Move the stick in every direction: it must not touch the edge of the hole.
STEP 3

Buttons and plate

4 buttons 12×12plate1 × M3×6
  1. Fit the caps on the buttons: yellow Y at the top, red B on the right, blue X on the left, green A at the bottom.
  2. Solder the signal wire to pin 1 of each button and the ground chain to pin 4 (diagonally opposite).
  3. Insert the buttons from the inside: the cap comes out of the square hole, the body goes into the 12.4 mm seat.
  4. Lay the plate on top: its legs go into the slots and the wires come out of the central holes.
  5. Fix it with the M3×6 screw in the central post and check that all 4 buttons click.
STEP 4

Upload the firmware and mount the Pico

Arduino IDERaspberry Pi PicoBOOTSEL
  1. In the Boards Manager install Raspberry Pi Pico/RP2040/RP2350 by Earle F. Philhower. No libraries needed: the USB gamepad is included.
  2. Before soldering, hold the Pico's BOOTSEL button while plugging in the cable: it shows up as the RPI-RP2 drive.
  3. Open controller_pico.ino, choose the Raspberry Pi Pico board and UF2_Board as the port, then Upload.
  4. Place the Pico in the cradle of the bottom shell, components facing up, with the USB-C socket in the rear window.
  5. Solder the wires into the pin holes from the component side, following the wiring table. Leave them long enough to open the shells like a book.
  6. Optional: a dab of hot glue at the bottom of the cradle. The socket is held anyway by the tooth on the top shell.

Later uploads do not need BOOTSEL: the IDE resets the Pico by itself over the USB port, even with the controller closed.

STEP 5

Test with the shells open

joy.cplserial monitor
  1. Plug in the USB cable without touching the stick: at start-up the firmware measures the joystick centre and the Pico LED blinks twice. The LED lights up while you press a button.
  2. On Windows press Win+R, type joy.cpl and open the controller Properties: check the 2 axes, the d-pad (POV) and the buttons.
  3. If an axis is reversed or swapped, change INVERT_X, INVERT_Y or SWAP_XY at the top of the sketch and upload again.
  4. To see the raw values open the serial monitor and send d.
STEP 6

Close the controller

4 × M3×122 × M3×8
  1. Route the wires away from the stick and the plate: no wire must lie under the joystick board.
  2. Rest the top shell on the centring lip of the bottom shell.
  3. From below, drive the 4 M3×12 screws into the body (two at the back, two at the front) and the 2 M3×8 into the grips: the shell is lower there and a long screw would come through the surface.
  4. Tighten crosswise without forcing: if the shells do not close, a wire is pinched.
STEP 7

Configure and play

RecalboxPC

With only 4 buttons, Start and Select are obtained by holding the joystick click (FUNCTION key):

CombinationFunction
L3 + YSTART
L3 + XSELECT (Recalbox hotkey)
L3 + AL1
L3 + BR1
L3 pressed and releasedL3 click
L3 held on its own for 5 secondsrecalibrates the stick centre
  1. Recalbox: plug the controller into a USB port of the Raspberry Pi. From the menu (Start) › Controller settings › Configure a controller, follow the prompts: for the d-pad move the stick (the firmware also sends a digital d-pad derived from the stick), for Start, Select and Hotkey use the combinations above.
  2. PC: Steam and most games see it as a generic gamepad; in Steam configure it once from Settings › Controller.
  3. If a game does not like the digital d-pad together with the stick, set DPAD_FROM_STICK to 0 in the sketch.
